---
title: "Geo erasure coding"
description: "Split a bucket's chunks into shards across sites instead of keeping a full copy at each: 1.5x storage for three sites, still readable with one site down, and a site's lost shards rebuilt from the others."
---

Normal replication keeps a **full copy** of every object at every site: three sites store three
times the data. Geo erasure coding cuts each chunk into Reed-Solomon shards and spreads them across
the sites instead.

With three sites and a 4+2 layout, each site keeps 2 of the 6 shards. Total storage is **1.5 times**
the data instead of 3 times, and objects stay readable when any one site is down.

## Requirements

- Every site has a **name**: from [site replication](/replication/site-replication), or from
  `Z3_SITE_NAME` on each node. Without a name, z3 refuses with `geo erasure coding needs a site name`.
- Every site of the bucket pulls **directly** from every other site: a mesh. Hub-and-spoke and chain
  topologies don't work here.
- At least two sites, and no site may hold more shards than the parity, so that losing one site
  always leaves enough shards. z3 refuses a layout that breaks this, with
  `a site would keep N shards but only P may be lost; raise parity or add sites`.
- At most 32 shards (`data + parity`), and at least as many shards as sites.

Without site replication, name each node with `Z3_SITE_NAME` and give each peer the same name as
that node's site, since the bucket's peers stand for its sites.

A site can be a [cluster](/deploy/cluster#geo-erasure-coding). Give every member the same
`Z3_SITE_NAME`, or none and use site replication. The cluster's shards are ordinary chunks placed on
its members, and cutting chunks down to shards and repair from other sites run on its leader.

## Turn it on

Set `geo` in the bucket's replication configuration. With site replication the peers follow the
topology, so `peers` can stay empty:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Console">
    Open the bucket, **Settings → Replication**, turn on geo erasure coding and choose data and
    parity shards.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Admin API">
    ```bash
    curl -X PUT http://127.0.0.1:9001/api/buckets/video/replication \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
      -d '{"peers": [], "geo": {"data": 4, "parity": 2}}'
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

It applies to **new writes**. Existing versions keep the layout they were written with (a full copy,
or an earlier geo layout).

### Choosing a layout

Shard `i` is kept by site number `i mod sites`, with the sites sorted by name.

| Sites | Layout | Shards per site | Storage | Survives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 2+2 | 2 | 2× | One site down |
| 3 | 4+2 | 2 | 1.5× | One site down |
| 4 | 6+2 | 2 | 1.33× | One site down |
| 4 | 4+4 | 2 | 2× | Two sites down |

## How it works

- **The layout travels with each version**, so every site agrees on who holds which shard, even
  after the bucket's configuration changes.
- **The writing site stores the full chunks first.** Once every other site has acknowledged the
  version (they then hold their shards), the background healer cuts the chunks into shards, keeps
  its own and deletes the full chunks.
- **Other sites never fetch full chunks**, only their own shards.
- **Reads** put each chunk together from the local shards plus shards fetched from other sites, and
  check it against its BLAKE3 hash. This adds the round trip between sites to a GET.
- **Shards are stored as ordinary chunks**, so on each site they're protected again by local
  [erasure coding](/durability/erasure-coding), [healing](/durability/healing) and garbage
  collection.
- Copying an object into a bucket without geo erasure coding stores its chunks in full again.

In tests with three sites, each site stored exactly 3 MiB for a 6 MiB object.

## Healing across sites

Local [erasure coding](/durability/erasure-coding) protects a site's shards against failed disks.
When this site's own shards are gone from **every** local copy, it rebuilds them from the shards the
other sites keep. That happens when:

- the site was rebuilt with empty disks, but its metadata was kept,
- every disk of a set was replaced,
- a shard is corrupt on every disk.

z3 fetches shards from the other sites, puts the chunk together with Reed-Solomon, checks it against
its BLAKE3 hash, and writes back only this site's shards. A full chunk the writing site still keeps
(not yet cut down to shards) is rebuilt the same way. No manual step is needed.

### When it runs

| Trigger | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **A full heal** | At the end of every full heal (`POST /api/heal`, `Z3_HEAL_INTERVAL`, a disk that comes back or is replaced), the shards it found missing or damaged are rebuilt |
| **Start** | About 10 seconds after start, missing shards only, without a full verification. A site that comes back with empty disks and its metadata fills up again by itself |
| **A read finds a bad shard** | The chunk is rebuilt shortly after (within 30 seconds) |
| **Retry** | While some chunks can't be rebuilt, again every `Z3_GEO_HEAL_RETRY` seconds (300 by default) |
| **On demand** | `POST /api/heal/geo` rebuilds missing shards now, without verifying every chunk |

A site rebuilt with **new** metadata (resynced from another site) gets its shards as it pulls the
versions, like any replica.

### Residency and traffic

- Objects that a [residency rule](/replication/bucket-replication#data-residency) keeps off this
  site are never fetched here; they're counted as `skipped`. The other sites check too: they refuse
  to send shards of such objects to an excluded site.
- Repair traffic follows each peer's [bandwidth limit](/replication/bucket-replication#bandwidth-limits-and-time-windows)
  (at low priority) and its time windows: a site outside its window isn't asked.
- The repair pass keeps to `Z3_HEAL_DUTY`, like a full heal.

### How many sites must answer

A chunk needs `data` shards to be put together. When too few sites answer, its chunks are reported
(not an error) and tried again on the next pass.

| Sites | Layout | Shards per site | To rebuild one site's shards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 4+2 | 2 | Both other sites must answer |
| 3 | 2+4 | 2 | One other site is enough |

A layout with more parity can rebuild from fewer sites, at the cost of more storage.

### Limits

- Versions in the [trash](/storage/deletion-protection) aren't repaired until they're restored.
- Sites running an older z3 without the peer route `POST /v1/geo/repair` are skipped, like a site
  that doesn't answer.
- In a [cluster](/deploy/cluster#geo-erasure-coding), this repair runs on the leader, and a shard
  counts as missing only when no member holds it. Call `POST /api/heal/geo` on the leader: on a
  follower it checks nothing.

## Watching it

`z3_replication_geo_chunks_total` on `/metrics` counts geo-coded chunks. The [Support](/operations/support)
page's **Inspect** shows how an object's chunks are stored on this node.

Repairs from other sites show in:

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| **Disks** page | Geo shards rebuilt from other sites, geo chunks not rebuildable now, the last geo check |
| `POST /api/heal`, `POST /api/heal/geo` | The `geo` result: `checked`, `damaged`, `repaired`, `failed`, `skipped`, `failed_chunks`, `errors` ([API](/api/admin-operations#disks-healing-and-garbage-collection)) |
| `GET /api/disks` | Healing totals: `geo_passes`, `geo_shards_repaired`, `geo_chunks_failed`, `geo_unrepairable`, `geo_skipped`, `last_geo_pass`, `last_geo_pass_ms` |
| Health check | The **Healing** item warns while some geo chunks can't be rebuilt |
| Prometheus | `z3_heal_geo_shards_repaired_total`, `z3_heal_geo_chunks_failed_total`, `z3_heal_geo_unrepairable` |

## When not to use it

- **Latency-sensitive reads.** Every GET needs shards from other sites.
- **Sites that are often unreachable.** Reads need enough sites up to gather `data` shards, and the
  writing site keeps full chunks until every site has acknowledged.
- **Small buckets.** The saving is in storage; for a little data, full copies are simpler.

For full copies at every site, use plain [bucket replication](/replication/bucket-replication) or
[site replication](/replication/site-replication).
